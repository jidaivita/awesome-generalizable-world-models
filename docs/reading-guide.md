# Reading Path

A sequence of twelve papers connecting representation analysis, prediction, planning, and adaptation. The guide assumes familiarity with representation learning and model-based reinforcement learning; its four sections can also be read independently.

## 01–03 · Representations and adaptation

These papers examine physical information in representations, learning from context, and tests of learned world structure.

### 01 · Interpreting Physics

**[Interpreting Physics in Video World Models](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/joseph26a.html)**

Sonia Joseph et al. · **ICML 2026**

Layer-wise linear probes, patch-level decoding, subspace analysis, and attention ablations locate physical information in video encoders. Motion direction forms a circular population code spread over many linear directions, while speed and acceleration become accessible earlier.

Probes and targeted steering characterize encoded physical variables and changes in decoded direction. These measurements concern representation geometry; they do not directly measure gains in closed-loop control.

**Connection:** Connects to SMDS through geometric analysis, with video motion variables replacing temporal concepts in language models.

[Topic overview: Representation Geometry & Analysis](modules/m2-geometry.md#paper-interpreting-physics)

### 02 · Context and Diversity Matter

**[Context and Diversity Matter: The Emergence of In-Context Learning in World Models](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/a46b9dcef23e9160f46d800a49a931d0-Abstract-Conference.html)**

Fan Wang et al. · **ICLR 2026**

The analysis separates environment recognition, which identifies a familiar environment, from environment learning, which estimates dynamics from the current context. Error bounds link these mechanisms to context length and training-environment diversity.

World-model experiments vary context, data diversity, and architecture to test the theoretical predictions. The distinction concerns how prediction improves with experience while model weights remain fixed.

**Connection:** Complements the first paper by testing adaptation from experience rather than the accessibility of a fixed physical variable.

[Topic overview: Generalization & Context-Based Adaptation](modules/m3-generalization.md#paper-context-diversity)

### 03 · What Has a Foundation Model Found?

**[What Has a Foundation Model Found? Using Inductive Bias to Probe for World Models](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/vafa25a.html)**

Keyon Vafa et al. · **ICML 2025**

Inductive-bias probes adapt a foundation model to synthetic tasks generated by a proposed world model. The adaptation behavior tests whether pretraining favors the hypothesized underlying structure.

Across several domains, strong training-task prediction can coexist with weak transfer of the proposed structure. Orbital-trajectory models provide a concrete test of transfer to new Newtonian-physics tasks.

**Connection:** Provides a behavioral counterpart to representation probes: adaptation to new tasks tests whether the proposed structure influences learning.

[Topic overview: Planning & Task-Relevant Representations](modules/m4-knowledge-use.md#paper-what-found)

## 04–05 · Geometry and prediction

SMDS compares geometric hypotheses; GRWM studies latent geometry and long-horizon prediction.

### 04 · SMDS / Shape Happens

**[Hypothesis-Driven Feature Manifold Analysis in LLMs via Supervised Multi-Dimensional Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01025)**

Federico Tiblias et al. · **TMLR 2026** · `LLM analysis`

Supervised Multi-Dimensional Scaling fits and compares candidate feature geometries, including circles, lines, and clusters. Temporal reasoning supplies a case study for testing how these structures change with context and support model outputs.

Experiments examine language-model families and sizes, held-out examples, and representation interventions. This is an analysis method from language modeling, included for its relevance to testing geometric hypotheses.

**Connection:** Adds explicit comparisons between geometric hypotheses to the representation-analysis methods in the first section.

[Topic overview: Representation Geometry & Analysis](modules/m2-geometry.md#paper-smds)

### 05 · GRWM

**[Cloning Deterministic Worlds: The Critical Role of Latent Geometry in Long-Horizon World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26782)**

Zaishuo Xia et al. · **CVPR 2026**

A temporal-context encoder and geometric regularization based on slowness and uniformity reshape the latent space before dynamics prediction. State-oracle comparisons and component ablations isolate representation quality from predictor capacity.

Long-horizon rollouts are evaluated in deterministic environments with fixed rules. The experiments distinguish geometric regularization from the contribution of temporal context.

**Connection:** Moves from diagnosing geometry to modifying a representation so that a learned predictor remains accurate over longer rollouts.

[Topic overview: Representation Geometry & Analysis](modules/m2-geometry.md#paper-grwm)

## 06–08 · World-model architectures and planning

DINO-WM, LeWorldModel, and JEPA-WMs examine visual encoders, predictive objectives, and planning algorithms.

### 06 · DINO-WM

**[DINO-WM: World Models on Pre-trained Visual Features enable Zero-shot Planning](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhou25t.html)**

Gaoyue Zhou et al. · **ICML 2025**

A dynamics predictor learns to forecast frozen DINOv2 spatial patch features from offline state-action trajectories. At test time, action sequences are optimized to bring predicted features close to the features of an image goal.

Six environments include varied maze layouts, pushing with different object shapes, and multi-particle settings. Goal-directed planning uses neither a learned reward model nor an inverse-dynamics model.

**Connection:** Establishes a planning interface in which an image goal becomes a target in a frozen visual feature space.

[Topic overview: Planning & Task-Relevant Representations](modules/m4-knowledge-use.md#paper-dino-wm)

### 07 · LeWorldModel

**[LeWorldModel: Stable End-to-End Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture from Pixels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19312)**

Lucas Maes et al. · **arXiv 2026**

The encoder and predictor train jointly from pixels with two losses: next-embedding prediction and a regularizer encouraging Gaussian-distributed embeddings. The resulting compact model supplies latent predictions for planning.

Experiments cover 2D and 3D control tasks, probes of physical quantities, and surprise responses to implausible events. The reported model has approximately 15 million parameters.

**Connection:** Contrasts with DINO-WM by learning the encoder jointly with the dynamics predictor instead of importing a frozen foundation encoder.

[Topic overview: Predictive Architectures & Pretraining](modules/m7-scaling.md#paper-leworldmodel)

### 08 · What Drives Success in JEPA-WMs?

**[What Drives Success in Physical Planning with Joint-Embedding Predictive World Models?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24497)**

Basile Terver et al. · **TMLR 2026**

A component study varies world-model architecture, training objective, and planning algorithm within the joint-embedding predictive family. The resulting configuration combines the empirically favorable choices.

Simulated environments and real robotic data support navigation and manipulation comparisons with DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC. The study attributes planning performance to specific modeling and optimization choices.

**Connection:** Places DINO-WM and related systems in a common component study, separating modeling choices from the planner used at evaluation.

[Topic overview: Predictive Architectures & Pretraining](modules/m7-scaling.md#paper-jepa-wms)

## 09–12 · Transfer and information gathering

These papers cover symmetry priors, changing dynamics, simulation-based policy evaluation, and task-informed exploration.

### 09 · Group-Structured Latent Space

**[Learning Abstract World Models with a Group-Structured Latent Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01529)**

Thomas Delliaux et al. · **arXiv 2025**

Known symmetries determine the latent manifold and the group actions used for transitions. Additional latent variables retain information that does not follow the prescribed symmetry.

Transition prediction and downstream reinforcement learning are tested in environments with translation and rotation, including first-person 3D views. The symmetry is supplied as prior knowledge.

**Connection:** Offers an architectural alternative to GRWM: symmetry determines the latent space and its transition operations.

[Topic overview: Representation Geometry & Analysis](modules/m2-geometry.md#paper-group-latents)

### 10 · Dynamics Are Learned, Not Told

**[Dynamics Are Learned, Not Told: Semi-Supervised Discovery of Latent Dynamics Geometries For Zero-Shot Policy Adaptation](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/xu26cq.html)**

Zhiming Xu et al. · **ICML 2026**

A trajectory encoder learns a dynamics context using a multi-positive InfoNCE objective. The analysis relates encoder smoothness to target-domain regret, motivating a latent geometry that supports adaptation without privileged physical-parameter labels.

MuJoCo experiments test severe dynamics shifts, including structural failures outside the explicit parameterization used by identification baselines, alongside in-distribution policy stability.

**Connection:** Connects latent geometry to dynamics adaptation, using trajectory-derived context instead of a supplied symmetry or physical-parameter label.

[Topic overview: Generalization & Context-Based Adaptation](modules/m3-generalization.md#paper-dynamics-geometries)

### 11 · SIMPLER

**[Evaluating Real-World Robot Manipulation Policies in Simulation](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/li25c.html)**

Xuanlin Li et al. · **CoRL 2024 (proceedings 2025)**

The benchmark reduces visual and control differences between real and simulated manipulation setups, then evaluates the same policies in both. Its goal is to reproduce policy comparisons and responses to distribution shifts.

More than 1,500 paired evaluations span two robot embodiments and eight task families. The reported evidence is agreement in real and simulated policy performance, rather than training a transferable dynamics model.

**Connection:** Changes the evaluation target from prediction or latent-space planning to agreement between simulated and real policy outcomes.

[Topic overview: Evaluation & Sim-to-Real Transfer](modules/m5-reliability.md#paper-simpler)

### 12 · Poke and Strike

**[Poke and Strike: Learning Task-Informed Exploration Policies](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/aoyama25a.html)**

Marina Y. Aoyama et al. · **CoRL 2025**

An exploration policy is rewarded according to how sensitive a privileged task policy is to errors in estimated object properties. An uncertainty criterion determines when to stop probing and execute the task.

Puck striking and CartPole analyze the task relevance of physical properties; a KUKA iiwa setup tests property estimation and adjusted execution on physical objects.

**Connection:** Closes the sequence with active information gathering: exploration is shaped by which property errors matter for the subsequent task.

[Topic overview: Exploration & Experience Replay](modules/m6-adaptation.md#paper-poke-strike)
